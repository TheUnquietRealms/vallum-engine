# The Unquiet Marches — Canon Guide

> **Canon source:** the finished manuscript *The Noise of Purpose* (Book One of
> *The Unquiet Marches*, by P A Myint). The manuscript is the single source of
> truth and is held local-only in the private content repo, not in this public
> repo.
>
> **Naming note (2026-06-14):** the world line was previously labelled "The
> Stormwright Cycle" across these docs. That name does not appear anywhere in the
> manuscript and has been retired. The series is **The Unquiet Marches**.
> Far-future mythology (the Stormwright pattern, the Aethers, the Ven Codex, the
> Turning) has been moved to the private world bible as *not-yet-canon* and must
> not be treated as current canon.

## What this guide is

This is the game-design extraction layer: the part of the canon Vallum needs to
build modules, scenes, character sheets, choices, map routes and rules
abstractions. It does not replace the manuscript.

## Adaptation principle

*The Unquiet Marches* is not generic fantasy. The core experience is not power
escalation, loot or heroic destiny. It is moral pressure, restraint, force,
consequence, political instability, and the cost of becoming capable of violence.

The game should let the player inhabit difficult decisions rather than simply win
encounters.

## Central playable question

What does it cost a person to become capable of great violence, and what does it
cost them to stop using that capacity as identity?

In game terms, the player should repeatedly face choices between capability,
restraint, obligation, withdrawal, reputation and consequence.

## Primary world frame

The playable geography is the Compact — a loose confederation of interior
lordships, eastern march garrisons, southern towns and northern territories,
stable enough to function but too fragmented to hold under deep pressure. Keep the
on-screen geography narrow: one road, one garrison or settlement, one threatened
community, one moral pressure point. The wider Compact should be felt, not
explained.

## Regions in play (Book One)

- **Eastern Marches** — military frontier; Kael's formation, contract violence,
  chain of command, the ridge above the caravan.
- **Interior lordships** — Caeden's keep; administrative coercion, commissions,
  reputational consequence (Reshen).
- **Garrison towns** — Sera's household, contract interludes.
- **Northern territories** — withdrawal, the road north, the stone chamber.

Mythological territory and theology are reserved for later books and held in the
private world bible (`future-arc-noncanon.md`). Do not introduce them in the
current line.

## The Surge and the Hollow

In Book One these are *experienced states*, not explained cosmology, and the game
must keep them that way.

- **The Surge** — heightened operational state. The world narrows, action becomes
  effortless, time moves differently. Seductive because it feels like clarity.
- **The Hollow** — what follows the Surge. A void. Kael has spent his life either
  chasing the Surge to avoid the Hollow, or learning to sit inside the Hollow
  without flinching. The book is the second education.

Do not give these supernatural explanation in the playable line. Earn it later.

## Core protagonist

**Kael Vorn**, called the **Iron Captain**. Not a conventional hero — a man whose
identity became legible through competence at violence. He notices terrain,
tactical shape, silence, threat and movement before he names emotion.

Core wound: **Reshen** — a valley cleared on Caeden's false commission.

Game stats (moral state, not a fighter class):

- **Force** — capacity to act decisively under threat (the Surge).
- **Restraint** — capacity not to use force when force is available (the gap).
- **Witness** — capacity to see accurately without self-protection (the
  cartographer's eye; the account).
- **Hollow** — accumulated psychic cost of action, inaction and moral evasion.
- **Reputation** — how the world reads him, whether or not he accepts the name.

## Key supporting characters (manuscript names)

- **Lord Caeden** — antagonist model: administrative competence, plausible
  language, clean hands. Never raises his voice; offers wine before the
  commission; never names the instrument.
- **Edric Vale** — cartographer. Precise, morally serious, compromised by what his
  maps enabled. Best in modules about evidence, routes, documents and the
  difference between accuracy and complicity.
- **Sera** — not a failed romance or villain. She loved something real in Kael but
  not the whole of him. Practical, self-possessed, unafraid of him.
- **Mael** — Caeden's intermediary. Carried the Reshen commission knowing what it
  meant. Negotiates culpability into something shared.
- **Davan** — young soldier, a parallel study in capability and the Hollow. Kael's
  past still walking around.

## Antagonist model

Antagonists begin not as monsters but as competent people using systems,
reputation, distance and delegated force. Lord Caeden is the model. Larger,
later-book antagonists are held in the private future-arc notes.

## Module design tone

Deliberate, restrained, morally pressurised. The best playable actions are not
attack/defend/flee/talk but choices such as:

- cut the head or protect the civilians
- ask the question or accept the commission
- write the letter or take up the sword
- withdraw or hide
- witness or intervene
- preserve order or expose cost
- act with certainty or act without certainty

## Visual and UX direction

Lean into maps, roads, chambers, storms, candlelight, documents, weather and
tactical stillness. The board is an ethical layout, not an arcade. Character
sheets should show moral state as much as combat state.

## First adaptation target

The first module, **The Noise of Purpose**, adapts Book One: the ridge above the
burning caravan, the choice between saving civilians and cutting the head, the
Surge, the raider captain, the Caeden commission, Reshen, Sera, the alley with
Mael, Davan at the crossroads, and the stone chamber.
