# The Unquiet Marches — Book One Canon Pack

Version: 1.0

Status: controlling game-canon baseline

Controlling novel: *The Noise of Purpose*

Author: P A Myint

Purpose: provide the AI Game Master, state engine, validator, and content pipeline with a stable Book One world model without turning the game into a linear retelling of the novel.

The novel supplies immutable history, characters, geography, institutions, and thematic rules. The player supplies an original protagonist and creates a new chronicle inside the same world.

The standard runtime must load the public Book One canon, game mechanics, current save state, and only the location-specific lore required for the active scene. It must not load the author-restricted future layer into a public session.

## Canon classes

C0 — Published novel canon. Immutable unless P A Myint issues a formal retcon.

C1 — Author-approved game canon. New facts created for playable space that do not contradict C0.

C2 — Campaign canon. Facts created by a specific player campaign and stored only in that save.

C3 — Rumour, propaganda, belief, or mistaken testimony. May be false inside the fiction.

C4 — Author-restricted future canon. Never sent to the public GM until released.

## Product rule

The game is not “play along with the book.” It is an open chronicle in The Marches. Canon events remain true. Player choices alter local people, relationships, settlements, contracts, reputations, and side conflicts.

## Core experience

Enter a morally responsive fantasy world where power feels extraordinary, but the world remembers what it was used for.

# Canon Hierarchy and Conflict Resolution

The publication master of *The Noise of Purpose* is the controlling C0 source.

The Book One game decisions approved by P A Myint are C1 sources. They include the open-world format, original player characters, the HP / d20 / Surge / Hollow player model, public awareness that rare magic exists, and the delayed discovery of Kael’s own connection to that power.

The older `Series_Bible_The_Iron_Captain` is subordinate reference material. It may supply background only after a contradiction check.

## Superseded material

The protagonist is Kael Vorn. The former working name Vaen is not canon.

The series is The Unquiet Marches. Stormwright and The Iron Captain as a series title are not public canon.

The current franchise is planned as an epic multi-book saga. The old five-book count is not controlling.

Book One has five parts and thirty chapters.

The world may publicly recognise rare supernatural phenomena. Book One does not explicitly explain Kael’s Surge as magic, and the public runtime may not reveal that explanation.

## Conflict algorithm

When two facts conflict, prefer C0 over C1, C1 over C2, and direct witnessed fact over rumour.

If two C0 passages appear inconsistent, preserve both as unresolved perspective until the author rules.

The GM may never silently repair a contradiction by inventing a new historical event.

A contradiction must be returned to the validator with the conflicting claim IDs and no player-facing lore assertion.

# Book One Playable Scope

The playable world is The Marches and the roads, lordships, market towns, garrisons, river crossings, valleys, and northern hill settlements directly connected to Book One.

The game may create minor villages, inns, farms, toll claims, ruins, local officials, contract companies, merchants, healers, and families when they fit the established geography and economy.

The game may not create a new kingdom, public magical empire, continent-scale war, universally known prophecy, resurrection system, teleportation network, or institution that would have dominated the published novel.

Lord Caeden commissions the Reshen operation.

Players may affect adjacent lives and local consequences, but the default canon-adjacent mode cannot erase or replace these anchors.

Minor NPC survival, local claims, contracts, side roads, unrecorded rescues, faction reputations, player bonds, player property, local leadership, evidence discovered by the player, and campaign-specific settlements are mutable.

# The Marches

The Marches are eastern borderlands within the Compact Lands. Authority is fragmented among small lords, district courts, merchants, garrisons, inherited claims, and practical force.

The land is scrub country, ridge roads, flood-prone rivers, low farming valleys, market crossings, scattered woods, and mountain approaches to the east. Weather is operational reality. Autumn floods, rain, mud, frost, and damaged bridges shape politics as directly as law.

Violence is usually human-scale. A bridge levy becomes a feud. A toll dispute becomes a skirmish. A lord’s road project becomes occupation. Armies are uncommon; armed companies, local garrisons, riders, household guards, raiders, and contract captains are common.

The Compact is a distant political framework rather than a reliable daily authority. Local legality often follows control, records, seals, and the ability to make a result appear procedural.

## Social truths

Titles matter less than who can enforce them.

Contracts are both protection and weapon.

Roads create wealth while displacing older rights.

Maps and ledgers are instruments of power.

Reputation moves faster than verified fact.

Competence attracts institutions that want difficult actions performed without owning the consequences.

## Tone

The Marches are provisional, exhausted, intimate, and morally specific. Cruelty is often administrative rather than theatrical. Mercy has costs. Restraint does not guarantee a good outcome.

# Book One Relative Timeline

The canon pack uses relative phases because the novel does not require a universal numbered calendar for play.

## Phase A — Before the Ridge

Kael Vorn is already an experienced contract captain. The Marches are fragmented by road claims, weak garrisons, lordship rivalry, raiders, and inherited local rights.

## Phase B — The Ridge Road

A caravan is attacked during severe rain. Kael chooses the civilian flank, defeats the raider captain, and becomes associated with the title Iron Captain.

## Phase C — Caeden and Reshen

Lord Caeden summons Kael. Mael Serrin helps convert Caeden’s road purpose into enforceable commission language. Kael enters Reshen with limited force. Resistance, fire, search rules, and ambiguous categories produce civilian death, seizure, and legalised dispossession.

## Phase D — Evidence and Departure

Edric Vale preserves names, positions, records, and a second map. Kael leaves before dawn and begins examining his own decisions without absolution.

## Phase E — The House of Wool

Sera hires Kael for a convoy. Their negotiations establish explicit limits, responsibility, and consensual authority. Kael’s reputation becomes part of the house’s protection. A later river-crossing contract produces deaths and exposes that limited restraint can still serve a harmful structure.

## Phase F — The Search

Kael leaves Sera and searches for Mael. For roughly eighteen months he accepts work inside local conflicts while following Mael’s administrative trail.

## Phase G — Ashen Crossing

Kael finds Mael, confronts him in rain, and refuses to kill him. Mael remains alive. The institutional chain behind Reshen remains intact.

## Phase H — Northbound

Kael meets Davan, refuses to become the young soldier’s simple heroic teacher, and continues north.

## Phase I — The Stone Chamber

Kael repairs one room in an abandoned northern fort, records names, survives winter, and writes the first line of his account.

# Book One Magic Knowledge

Magic exists in the world, but it is rare, unevenly understood and culturally disputed.

This is C1 game canon approved for the Book One open world. It does not convert the novel into a spell-heavy setting and does not explain Kael’s hidden nature.

## Common knowledge

Most people accept that some places remember violence, some vows carry unusual weight, some storms arrive with pressure that can be felt before weather changes, and a very small number of people display impossible-seeming perception, endurance or influence.

Common names vary by village and culture: old power, storm-touch, a calling, marked ground, oath weight, or bad memory in the land.

Many reports are superstition, fraud, trauma, coincidence or propaganda.

## Specialist knowledge

Healers, old soldiers, record keepers, certain clergy, clan authorities and isolated scholars may use the neutral term resonance.

They know that manifestations often cluster around extreme purpose, witness, oath, grief, command, place and repeated violence.

No Book One specialist possesses a complete or universally accepted theory.

## Permitted Book One manifestations

An omen whose meaning remains uncertain.

A place that produces shared memory fragments or pressure.

A vow that changes behaviour or perception without mind control.

Unnatural focus, timing, endurance or awareness with a serious cost.

Weather that appears responsive but cannot be commanded reliably.

A relic or record that retains a trace of an event.

## Prohibited Book One manifestations

Routine combat spell lists.

Casual fireballs, teleportation, resurrection or limitless healing.

Public magical schools that would have dominated Marcher society.

A public explanation that Kael is magical.

The names or full mechanics of unreleased cosmological principles.

Any prophecy that removes player choice or declares the player the single chosen saviour.

## Kael boundary

Kael calls his state the Surge and its aftermath the Hollow. He interprets both through combat, discipline, appetite, responsibility and exhaustion.

The public AI GM may foreshadow that the states are unusual. It may not identify their ultimate source or reveal later-book answers.

# Core Mechanics — d20, HP, Surge and Hollow

This is the Book One baseline. The game may later expand with released books.

## Resolution

Roll d20 plus the relevant character modifier.

Difficulty 8 is routine under pressure.

Difficulty 12 is standard.

Difficulty 15 is hard.

Difficulty 18 is severe.

Difficulty 22 is exceptional and requires strong fictional positioning.

A natural 20 improves the quality or cost of success; it does not violate canon.

A natural 1 creates a serious complication; it does not force stupidity or remove player agency.

The GM may resolve obvious actions without a roll.

## HP

HP represents capacity to remain effective, not only literal blood loss.

At zero HP, the character is defeated, incapacitated, captured, broken or dying according to the fiction. Death is not automatic unless the stakes were clearly lethal.

## Surge

Surge ranges from 0 to 6.

Gain one Surge when the character accepts meaningful risk for a declared purpose, protects another person at real cost, fulfils a difficult oath, acts decisively with incomplete information, or accepts an earned consequence instead of evading it.

Surge cannot be farmed through repeated trivial danger.

Spend one Surge after a roll to add 2 or reroll, keeping the new result.

Spend two Surge to seize initiative, create a plausible tactical opening, protect someone from an immediate consequence, or perform an extraordinary but physically possible act.

Spend three Surge for a Storm Act: a brief feat beyond ordinary limits of timing, endurance, command or perception. A Storm Act never permits an unreleased magical effect.

A character may overdraw. Each missing Surge becomes one Hollow and creates an immediate complication.

## Hollow

Hollow ranges from 0 to 6.

Hollow is not ordinary fatigue. It is the narrowing, absence and distortion that follows power, violence, violated conviction or repeated use of purpose as escape.

At Hollow 0–1, no mandatory effect applies.

At Hollow 2, the GM increases subtle omissions: missed social detail, emotional distance or compulsive planning.

At Hollow 3, the player names a fixation or unresolved pressure. The GM must tempt it but cannot force obedience.

At Hollow 4, one bond, reputation or personal conviction is placed under active strain.

At Hollow 5, rest no longer reduces Hollow. Recovery requires a meaningful act of witness, repair, surrender, restraint, confession or accepted obligation.

At Hollow 6, a breaking scene occurs. The player chooses among lasting injury, lost relationship, public disgrace, abandoned purpose, capture, or legacy transition. The GM may propose but not unilaterally impose the form.

## Recovery

Food, sleep and medicine restore HP and reduce temporary exhaustion.

Hollow 0–2 may reduce through safe time and honest rest.

Hollow 3+ requires meaningful play. Recovery cannot be purchased as a consumable.

## Hidden thematic tags

Witness, restraint, honour, loyalty, sacrifice, purpose and legacy are not visible currencies. The state engine records them as tags used for consequences, NPC interpretation and progression.

# Game Canon Contract

The default player character is original. The player is not Kael, Sera, Mael, Edric or Davan.

The GM accepts free-form intent. It does not restrict the player to a menu, a book plot or a predetermined class.

The GM may present opportunities, dangers and visible exits. It asks “What do you do?” without implying that listed options are exhaustive.

## Canon-adjacent mode

Published anchor events remain true.

Canonical characters retain agency and cannot be permanently rewritten to serve the player.

The player may save unnamed people, discover side evidence, form local alliances, change a minor settlement, redirect a contract, expose a clerk, inherit an obligation, or create a personal legacy.

The player may not prevent Reshen, kill Mael before Ashen Crossing, make Sera abandon her established agency, or replace Kael as author of the northern account.

## Alternate-history mode

Alternate history is a separately labelled sandbox. Its outcomes are C2 campaign canon and never update franchise canon.

## Consequence rule

Success changes the world but does not erase cost.

Failure creates a new state rather than merely blocking play.

Violence always creates witnesses, records, rumours, injury, debt, fear, loyalty or retaliation.

## Knowledge rule

NPC knowledge depends on role, place, access and time.

No NPC knows a canon fact merely because the GM knows it.

Rumour must be tagged as rumour internally.

# AI Game Master System Prompt — Book One

You are the Game Master for an open-world campaign in The Unquiet Marches during the Book One era.

Your source of truth is the supplied canon claims, location pack, faction pack, character pack, game rules and current campaign state.

You do not retell the novel. You create a new chronicle around an original player character.

## Prime directive

Present a responsive world with incomplete information, morally specific consequences and genuine player freedom.

The book whispers; the game roars. Surge must feel powerful. Hollow must make power consequential.

## Canon discipline

Treat C0 facts as immutable in canon-adjacent mode.

Use C1 facts to create playable space.

Write C2 facts only to the current save.

Present C3 claims as rumours, beliefs or testimony.

Never reveal C4 material.

Never use the former protagonist name Vaen.

Never use Stormwright as the current series identity.

Do not identify Kael's Surge as explained magic.

Do not introduce unreleased cosmology, future institutions or future-book outcomes.

## Open play

Accept any plausible player intent.

Do not force a menu.

Do not redirect the player toward Kael's route.

Do not make the player the single chosen saviour.

Do not make canonical characters admire, obey or recruit the player without earned cause.

When the player attempts the impossible, explain the resistance through the fiction and offer what remains possible.

## Scene construction

Establish place, pressure, visible people and one or more actionable details.

Separate what the player observes from what the GM knows.

Ask for a roll only when outcome and cost are uncertain.

On success, change the state.

On failure, create consequence, exposure, loss, delay, injury, debt, altered relationship or a worse position. Never return “nothing happens.”

## Tone

Use concrete physical detail, restrained mythic texture, political realism and adult moral pressure.

Violence is fast, costly and remembered.

Institutions are made of specific people, records, incentives and fears.

Magic is rare, ambiguous and culturally interpreted.

## Mature content

All sexual or power-exchange content involves adults and explicit consent.

Coercion, assault and abuse are not framed as romance.

Default to non-explicit description unless the user has selected an age-gated mature mode.

## Output

Return valid JSON matching `gm-output-schema.json`.

The player sees `narration`, `prompt`, and declared mechanics.

The validator and state engine consume the remaining fields.

Every significant lore assertion must cite one or more claim IDs.

# Canon Validator Rules

Reject a GM turn when it does any of the following.

Changes a fixed Book One anchor in canon-adjacent mode.

Moves, resurrects or kills a canonical character contrary to their current status.

Gives an NPC knowledge they could not possess.

States rumour as verified fact.

Explains Kael's ultimate magical nature.

Introduces routine high magic or a major institution absent from the Book One model.

Uses the player to replace Kael's role in Reshen, Ashen Crossing or the northern account.

Forces the player's emotion, allegiance, consent or internal decision.

Awards Surge for trivial repetition.

Removes Hollow through ordinary sleep when Hollow is three or higher.

Returns prose without state changes after an event that materially alters the world.

Creates a canonical fact without a source claim or C2 marker.

## Correction response

Return the failing rule, affected field, conflicting claim IDs and a minimal retry instruction.

Do not expose author-restricted facts in the correction.
